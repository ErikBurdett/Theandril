# Theandril city-states

## What a city-state is in this world

A city-state is one settlement that never joined anything. When the Witness Roads failed in RR 2311 and the Ashfall followed two years after, the hearthlands did not break cleanly into four powers and then twenty-four; they broke into every town that could still feed itself. Most of those towns were absorbed, burned out, or walked away from. These twenty-four were not. Each keeps a single work — a crossing, a seam, a cistern, a roll of names — that the great realms would rather use than replace, and each has made refusing membership into a civic habit as old as its walls.

They are not new cultures. A city-state's households speak the language, wear the cloth, work the crafts and raise the troops of whichever registered culture its region belongs to; it borrows that culture's art and unit roster entire. What is its own is the name over the gate, the colour on the banner, the word cut above the door, and the particular bargain it refuses to sign. In the Compact's phrase, they keep the hearth without keeping anyone else's oath.

The register below follows the [faction bible](FACTION_BIBLE.md) in what it claims. These are small powers of the Age of Fracture, described at the same reference present of **RR 2447**. None of them is an unremembered great realm, a surviving crown, or a secret custodian of the Roads. Where a city-state's story touches the Book's attested events — the Failing, the Ashfall, the collapse of the old League, the fall of the Null Throne — it touches them as a survivor, not as a witness with new testimony.

## Harbours and shores

### Cullethaven

`citystate.cullethaven` — borrows **Glass Tide** — ***We sell the wreck, not the story.***

Cullethaven keeps the cutting yards: a mile of sheds on the vitrified coast where Ashfall glass is bought by weight, cut, and sold on without any question about which beach it came off. It refuses exclusive charters of every kind, having watched what the old paper sea did to towns that signed them, and it keeps the harbour open to hostile flags on the same tide. A conqueror takes a deep anchorage and a year's stacked cullet; what he does not take is the scavengers, who have never once landed at a quay where their cargo could be seized, and who will beach it forty miles up the coast instead.

### Bittern Quay

`citystate.bittern_quay` — borrows **Saltwind Remnant** — ***Old debts drowned with old ships.***

The pans came first and the town grew around them: low tarred sheds, flats of drying salt, and crews who walked off wrecked charters when the League went down and declared themselves clear of their owners' books. Bittern Quay pledges a keel for one voyage and will not sign for two, which the surviving charter households call theft and the quay calls arithmetic. Take it and you hold salt the hearthlands cannot do without, garrisoned by people whose founding act was abandoning an owner — a habit that does not improve under a new one.

### Wrackpool

`citystate.wrackpool` — borrows **Brine Choir** — ***Answer the shore that answers you.***

Wrackpool is built on a warm shelf of tidal pools that yield salt, shellfish and a dye made nowhere else, tended by brinefolk and shore households who answer every proposal together in open water-meeting. It refuses permanent quays, because a quay encloses a pool and a pool belongs to the shelf. A conqueror gains the pans easily enough and loses the dye within two seasons: the tending is daily, seasonal and unwritten, and the tenders can simply stop going down to the water.

## Rivers, fens and channels

### Loamferry

`citystate.loamferry` — borrows **Reedbound Council** — ***Both banks pay the same.***

Loamferry holds the only dependable crossing for forty miles and charges either bank the same, in coin, grain or work, which is the whole of its constitution and most of its diplomacy. It refuses to be anyone's bridgehead; twice it has cut its own cables rather than carry a column that would not state its destination. Whoever takes the landings inherits a channel that silts, moves and lies, and the pilots who could read it have gone downstream to a village with the sense to stay quiet.

### Willowmere

`citystate.willowmere` — borrows **Mire Courts** — ***The water decides. We only record it.***

A ring of stilt halls around a slow mere, Willowmere still keeps the season-court forms its old fen landlords used, and applies them with great care to those landlords' descendants. It refuses permanent title anywhere the shoreline moves, hears every claim at flood and enforces none until the water drops. The mere swallows siege lines as reliably as it swallows boundary stakes; a conqueror gains damp halls and the one thing actually worth having there, which is the town's complete record of who drained what and when.

### Lockmouth

`citystate.lockmouth` — borrows **Red Sluice Directorate** — ***Whoever holds the gates owes the fields.***

At the head of a lowland channel network, Lockmouth is run by a board of gate-keepers elected by the households the water actually reaches, which is not the same as the households that pay for it. It refuses schedules set by anyone downstream who does not do the digging. Once it opened the gates to drown an approaching column and has been arguing about the ruined fields ever since. The gates can be taken in a morning; the maintenance cannot, and an unworked channel silts in a single season and takes the lowland harvest down with it.

### Ninearch

`citystate.ninearch` — borrows **Wardhall Remnant** — ***Let the span be inspected.***

Ninearch grew on and around a nine-arched bridge its engineers inspect in public every spring, reading the faults aloud before they read the accounts. It will raise a road, a mole or a siege ramp for whoever pays the survey, and refuses to raise the same work twice for two sides of one war — a rule it has broken once and does not discuss. It also refuses the old Throne's claim that reconstruction needs royal authority. A conqueror gains the crossing and the inspection roll, which will tell him, in a clerk's hand, precisely which piers he has spent the siege undermining.

## Holds of the high country

### Highseam

`citystate.highseam` — borrows **Iron Covenant** — ***One gate. One price. No hurry.***

Highseam closed its upper passes a century ago and has traded finished ironwork through one gate on set days ever since. It quotes a price and holds it for a year, refuses inspection of its workings, and has never told any buyer how deep the seams run or how much iron is stacked behind the gate. It survives because it can shut, and everybody knows it can shut. Take the valley and you must hold it through a winter on supply lines the hold itself used to keep open — and the lower galleries will be flooded before the gate comes down.

### Pyrefell

`citystate.pyrefell` — borrows **Cinder March** — ***Dig where the fire finished.***

Pyrefell cuts ore out of ground the Ashfall buried, in galleries that any given spring may or may not still exist, and lights its beacon line every night from a habit nobody now defends on grounds of use. It sells to any banner and admits none of them past the gate, having concluded early that a garrison is a mine-master with soldiers. Its seams are real and a conqueror gets them; its crews know which workings are sound and which are held up by ash, and that knowledge has never once been written down.

### Hoarhold

`citystate.hoarhold` — borrows **Rimehorn Clans** — ***Winter counts the guests.***

A stone hold above the snowline keeping common stores for six clans, Hoarhold opens them to any traveller under a rule older than the Failing: shelter for as long as the storm lasts, and not one day longer. It refuses to let a guest become a garrison, and has fed enemies through a blizzard and met them in the field at the thaw without considering either act remarkable. A conqueror gains a hold that eats more than it makes, supplied over passes that close; the clans winter elsewhere and come back when the siege has starved.

### Windlass Ledge

`citystate.windlass_ledge` — borrows **Cairnwing Concord** — ***Nothing rises for free.***

Every sack of grain and every dressed stone on the red escarpment is raised by the windlass crews of the Ledge, cairnfolk and human households working the same cordage in the same shifts. The lift rate is set at open hearing, and the crews refuse to lift an armed column at any rate, which is why the Ledge has been besieged twice and taken never. A conqueror who reaches the top gains a town with the precise problem the town has already solved, and no crews: the crews are the lift, and crews can climb down.

## Roads, halts and companies

### Grasshalt

`citystate.grasshalt` — borrows **Sable Steppe** — ***Camp here. Do not settle here.***

Grasshalt is a permanent halt on the open grass, a stone ring and a fair calendar, where camp households meet to trade horses, hides and news under the rule that no company pitches inside the ring twice in a season. It holds good pasture it refuses to plough, and refuses equally the argument that a walled market may speak for the camps that use it. Take it and you have walls; the fair will be held sixty miles east next spring, and the walls will be standing in empty grass.

### Dunwell

`citystate.dunwell` — borrows **Cistern Assembly** — ***Drink at the measure, not above it.***

Three deep cisterns under a dry-basin caravan stop, and a posted measure read aloud each morning to householders and guests alike. Dunwell refuses to sell a season's whole draw to any one buyer, and has watered armies on both sides of the same campaign without offering an opinion on either. It survives because everyone crossing the basin needs it whole. A conqueror gains the wells and inherits the measure whether he honours it or not: draw past it and the basin is dry long before the rains.

### Sixways

`citystate.sixways` — borrows **Manytrack Moot** — ***Six roads in. No road owned.***

Six old roads meet at a timber moot-ground where horned, furred and human households hear one another's claims in the open and go home the same night. Sixways keeps no wall on the road side, refuses to fence any approach, and has never settled which of its gates is the front one. It survives because six open roads are worth more to everyone than one closed junction. A conqueror gains the junction and loses the hearings, and traffic that has been avoiding tolls since the Long Ash moves quietly to the fords.

### Tollfast

`citystate.tollfast` — borrows **Unsealed Companies** — ***Paid work ends. Debts should end too.***

A company hold in a rebuilt toll fort on a ruined road corridor, where veterans hire out by the season and let every contract lapse at its term, on principle and in writing. Tollfast collects no toll — the name is a joke about the fort's previous owners — refuses to inherit anyone's oath, and keeps the only honest account of what the corridor's fighting has actually cost. Taking the fort is easy and gains a fort. The companies were the asset, and men who cancel contracts as a matter of belief make poor garrisons for anyone.

## Kilns, fields and galleries

### Bellowstead

`citystate.bellowstead` — borrows **Ashen Compact** — ***Every mark answers for its maker.***

Bellowstead is kiln courts and bellows yards governed by the masters whose marks are struck into the work, and a failed piece is repaired by the household that made it, without argument and at its own cost. It swears no oath it cannot repair, and refuses every charter that would put its workshops under a council it cannot walk to in a morning. A conqueror gains the kilns, the ash-glaze clay and the reusable forms; the masters leave with their marks, their moulds and their apprentices, and a captured Bellowstead makes pots.

### Quickfallow

`citystate.quickfallow` — borrows **Emberwake Convocation** — ***Ash is only soil that is late.***

In the outer ash belts, Quickfallow keeps seed rooms holding varieties nobody else kept and plants them out each year in test strips across the grey ground. It refuses both the counsel to abandon the ash country and the teaching that the fire was holy, and gives seed to neighbours it has no strength to defend, keeping only the account of who brought seed back. A conqueror gains stores and loses the strips: the keepers scatter, and thirty years of knowing which grain will take in burnt ground goes with them.

### Hollowlease

`citystate.hollowlease` — borrows **Underhush Exchange** — ***Trade quietly or trade elsewhere.***

A market grown into the shallow galleries beneath a wooded escarpment, where every stall holds a lease with clauses on smoke, noise and repair, and where more residents live than traders sell. Hollowlease refuses any lease that would let a buyer silence the people who live over the stalls, and it evicts by vote when a tenant's trade shakes the ceiling. Its vaults are the driest storage in the region. They are held up by maintenance rather than masonry, and the wards that do the maintenance can stop.

### Duskpress

`citystate.duskpress` — borrows **Vesper Court** — ***The vintage is owed. Nothing else is.***

The vineyard households of Duskpress bought out their estate's inherited provisions within living memory and now owe it wine and nothing further, a bargain kept on public view at the press-house door. They keep the shuttered houses at the head of the valley in good repair — this is policy, not affection — and refuse every proposal that would make the old provisions negotiable again. A conqueror gains vines and a bargain he was not party to; neither were the patrons above the town, and they were there first.

## Keepers of records and refuge

### Chalkvigil

`citystate.chalkvigil` — borrows **Sepulchral Synod** — ***Count them all. Name them all.***

Cut into a chalk terrace, Chalkvigil's ossuaries hold the named dead of a dozen towns that no longer exist, and its keepers read a portion of the roll aloud every day of the year. It charges for burial and never for the reading, and refuses to release a name to anyone who wants it to prove an inheritance — which is why half the hearthlands' great households both fund it and resent it. A conqueror gains vaults he will not want opened and keepers who need only stop reading; an unread roll unmakes a dozen towns a second time.

### Candlerest

`citystate.candlerest` — borrows **Lantern Hospices** — ***The door is open. The knife stays outside.***

A walled hospice town at a road junction, keeping clean water, dry beds and a lit door for anyone who arrives unarmed. Candlerest treats the wounded of every banner, refuses to bar a road or hold a prisoner, and has buried enough of both sides to have stopped asking whom the dead served. It survives because every realm in reach expects to need it. Taking it gains a full storehouse and costs something no army recovers: the attendants go, the water discipline goes with them, and the wards fill with fever inside a month.

### Lodestair

`citystate.lodestair` — borrows **Velvet Meridian** — ***A measure is public or it is a rumour.***

An observatory town on a dry upland stair, where the night's readings are posted in the market by morning and any reader may challenge them in front of the observers who took them. Lodestair sells copies to all comers and has twice refused, publicly and expensively, to sell a patron exclusive use of a measurement. Its tables are in every serious wayfinder's satchel. A conqueror gains the instruments and loses the readings, because the observers can go on working and simply stop posting, and an unchallenged table is worth nothing to anyone.

### Endleaf

`citystate.endleaf` — borrows **Margin Observance** — ***Keep the blank page as well.***

The smallest of the twenty-four: an archive station at the cold edge of settled country, staffed in rotation by custodians who copy what they are given and file the gaps with the same care as the text. Endleaf keeps sealed cases it refuses to open for payment or threat, and posts the exact number of them in the entry hall, so that nobody need wonder what it is hiding — only what is in it. A conqueror gains shelves, a list he did not write, and custodians who burn nothing and explain nothing.

### Spindlewood

`citystate.spindlewood` — borrows **Morrow Spore** — ***Shelter is not a debt.***

Spindlewood is a refuge that grew into a town beneath the underwood canopy, settled by households that fled three separate collapses and agreed, early and permanently, not to question one another about the fourth. Admission costs a year of work; nobody arriving without a banner is turned away; nobody claimed by an old lord is handed back. It is hard to march through and poor to loot. A conqueror gains timber and finds the town gone into the underwood in a night, where a siege line means precisely nothing.

## What these are not

> [!warning]
> City-states are map inhabitants, not playable seats. None of them has its own art: each is drawn with the existing approved assets of the registered culture it borrows, tinted to its own banner colour, and each fields that culture's ordinary unit roster at the ordinary shared costs. Nothing above grants a new command, a special rule, a unique unit, an exclusive project or a supernatural aptitude. A city-state's stated refusal — of charters, of garrisons, of inherited debt — is its lore, not a mechanic that binds a player: it holds a settlement, works claims, raises armies, withstands sieges and falls to them like anything else on the map. The canonical definitions live in [city-states](../../packages/content/src/city-states.ts); the registered cultures they borrow from live in [factions](../../packages/content/src/factions.ts) and [the faction bible](FACTION_BIBLE.md).
