> Before the first stone was sealed, the world already owed itself a debt. — carving above the drowned door at Cold Ford, author unknown

Every people in Theandril keeps a story of the beginning, and every story contradicts the others, and I have outlived enough theologians to say plainly: **no account of the world's making is attested by any witness.** I record the three tellings that held the widest allegiance in the old world, and I rank them by the number of their believers, not by their truth, which is not mine to weigh.

## The three tellings

- **The Kiln.** The mountain peoples — the deep-folk of the Anvilheights foremost — hold that the world was fired like a vessel in a kiln, and came out flawed, and that the flaw runs through everything: every vein of ore, every heart. Work, in this telling, is worship: to forge, to mend, to shore up the crack. The Iron Covenant built two thousand years of doctrine on it.
- **The First Bargain.** The hearth peoples of the river plains taught that the world began as an agreement between the land and the dead — the living permitted to plant and build in exchange for remembrance. Break the remembering, and the lease fails. After the Ashfall, you will understand, this telling acquired a terrible new congregation.
- **The Drowned Star.** Along the coasts they said a star fell burning into the first sea, and everything that lives crawled out of the scald. The fen courts kept a crueler version: that the star is still down there, still burning, and the tides are its breathing. Sailors of the Saltwind League swore by it. Their descendants on the Glass Coast have particular reasons, now, to remember it.

## What can be attested

The oldest things in Theandril that a sober witness can touch are these: the **waystones before the Roads** — raw standing stones on hilltops and fords, older than any nation, already ancient when the Witnessry claimed them; the **bone terraces** of the southern chalklands, burial architecture on a scale no living people could explain, which the Sepulchral Synod would later inherit and put to use; and the **fourteen springs of magic**, which are not places but inheritances — the traditions of Flame and Storm, Tide and Stone, Verdancy and Grave, Star and Dream, Shadow and Radiance, Spirit and Rune, Oath, and the one we do not name in charity, the Void. No one taught the first peoples these arts. They were found in the blood and the land the way a river is found: already running. Their natures are set out in the [[Appendix — The Fourteen Traditions]].

Of the elder ancestries I will say only what I have seen or copied. The deep-folk of the mountains are attested in every age, patient and closed. The fen-courts of the Deepfen are older than their own records and pleased to keep it so. Giants are attested in three reliable accounts and several hundred unreliable ones. The dead are attested everywhere, always, in every ledger of the world; whatever else changes, the dead accumulate. And there were things in the far places — the high cold, the deep dark, the wide water — that never sent an envoy or signed a bargain, and hold their domains to this hour.

> [!warning]
> A marginal note, in my own later hand: I have been asked, by kings and by children, what *I* believe of the beginning. I believe the world is old, and owed, and that nobody alive holds the note. Beyond that I decline to testify.
