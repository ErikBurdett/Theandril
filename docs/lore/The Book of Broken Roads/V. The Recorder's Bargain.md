> You are asking to be the last man at every deathbed. — the Provost's examiner, at my petition; the one sentence of the interview I was permitted to keep

Here I set down my own entry, at its place in the sequence, no longer and no more tender than the age deserves. A history from the mouth of a lich owes the reader an accounting of the mouth.

## The mortal ledger

Born RR 2183 in the toll-town under Grey Weir, on the river Sallow, third child of a weigh-clerk. The ash-lung took my brothers in the outbreak of 2191; it spared me with nothing worse than the wheeze I carried until I no longer needed breath. Apprenticed to the station at fourteen — a boy who could cut a straight seal was fed, and my father knew it. Sworn Witness at twenty. Third Recorder at twenty-seven, under First Recorder Maude, who taught me the whole of my craft and most of my conscience. I married a boatwright's daughter, Senna, in RR 2212. I record her name because she deserves a stone somewhere, and I am the only stone that held.

In RR 2229 Maude was hanged, as set down in [[IV. The Age of Crowns]], for refusing to falsify the record. I stood in the crowd with the station's ledgers strapped to my back — her last instruction — and I understood two things before her feet stopped. First: the order would not protect the record. Second: the record would die anyway, hanged or honored, because records are kept in meat, and meat fails. Every archive in the world was one fire, one flood, one dishonest generation from silence. The thought did not leave me. Thoughts like that do not leave; they nest.

## The Second Interment

I petitioned the Ninth Terrace in RR 2230. I will not describe the year of examination except to say the Synod refuses more petitions than it grants and is right to, and that they asked me one question in a hundred forms: *what do you wish to keep?* Men who answer 'my life' are refused. Men who answer 'my power' are refused and watched. I answered 'the ledger,' every time, in every form, because it was true, and in the spring of RR 2231 I was granted the rite the terraces call the Second Interment.

Of the rite itself: they bury you alive with your own testament, and what has truly finished with living climbs back out with it. The body dies honestly — I insist on this point against every fireside version — the heart stops, the blood settles, the rot begins and is *arrested*, bound and inked and sealed in place by Grave-craft and Rune-craft and a Forbidden art whose name I hold under oath. What returns is not the man preserved but the man **notarized**: a fair copy of the soul, cut into its own bone. It hurt more than the ash-lung and less than the hanging I watched. I climbed out on the third day with my testament in my teeth, and I have not slept since, and the dark of the grave has never entirely finished being my weather.

## What it cost

- Taste, temperature, sleep, and the wheeze. I miss the wheeze. It was my brothers'.
- My orders. The Witnessry struck me from the rolls within the season — *a Witness must be capable of erasure*, ran the judgment, *and this man has made himself unerasable.* I have had a century to appreciate that they were right by their own law, and I keep their law better than they kept it, so I bear the expulsion lightly.
- Senna. She lived to seventy-one and would not see me after the rite, and she was right too. I did not attend her burial in RR 2266. I recorded it. If the reader wants a definition of what I am, it is there.
- Standing with the living. To the hearthfolk I am Ledgerbone, a horror that files. They are not wrong. A lich is a Forbidden thing, and the tradition that made me has drowned villages in its time; I do not petition for the rehabilitation of my kind. I petition for the record, only ever for the record.

> [!note]
> Why the Synod grants such rites at all, I do not know and do not guess; this book does not deal in motives, mine excepted, and mine are on the table. I was made to remember. Everything after the spring of RR 2231 in this book, I witnessed with these eyes or copied from a named source. Weigh me accordingly.
