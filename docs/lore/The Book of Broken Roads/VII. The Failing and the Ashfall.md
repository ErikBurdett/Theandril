> On the morning of the last day of RR 2311, forty thousand bargains stood attested across the world. By midwinter the number was none.

Two years broke the world: 2311 and 2313. I witnessed both from the tower at Grey Weir, which I had held alone since the chapter fled, and I ask the reader's patience if this chapter's entries are shorter than the age deserves. They were short years. They simply had no bottom.

## The Quiet — RR 2311

The Failing was not an event but a cascade, and its first hours have a hundred claimed causes; I give the three with named sources and let them stand in their contradiction, as my vow requires. A Covenant survivor-roll blames the Throne: a final massed anchor-working meant to silence the Synod's terraces at a stroke. A Synod instrument — I hold a certified copy — blames the harvesters: the lattice's *attending strength*, drawn past some floor no one had ever sounded. A League pilot's log, the humblest and to my eye the most honest, records only that the Anchorage stone went quiet at dawn, mid-attestation, 'like a man dying between one word of the sentence and the next.'

What is attested past dispute is the sequence: the great junction stones went first, and every stone that had routed its speech through them found itself alone within days. Stations did not shatter or burn. They went *quiet* — the seals still cut, the wax still cooled, and nothing answered from anywhere. Within a season, the entire architecture of distant trust in Theandril had ceased to exist. Every bargain became local. Every stranger became a stranger again. Credit — forty times the world's worth of it — did not unwind; it simply vanished, and every granary, army, marriage, treaty, and debt that had stood on it stood, for one long teetering moment, on nothing.

## The collapse of the powers

- **The Saltwind League** died of arithmetic in a winter. Fleets whose keels were pledged paper simply changed masters at sword-point, harbor by harbor. The Anchorage burned in the spring riots. Aldery the Debt-Crowned was put over the side of his own flagship by his creditors, wearing, the pilot's log records, his full chain of office. It does not record whether the chain was weighed first. It would have been like them to weigh it.
- **The Null Throne** held longest, as ward-lines and granaries will, and fell to its own doctrine: with the stones quiet, Wardhall's word could not out-travel rumor, and a throne built on distrust of unseen powers was poorly placed when it became, to every distant province, an unseen power. The Middle Reach shattered into warlord marches within a decade. Ossric's line ended obscurely, which the reader may find either fitting or unbearable; I find it both.
- **The Iron Covenant** sealed the passes — their oldest reflex — and the record with them. What has come out of the Anvilheights since the Failing is trade-goods, closed faces, and no answers. I do not attest the Covenant fell. I attest that the mountains closed, and hold.
- **The Mire Courts** had withdrawn in RR 2289, and the Deepfen endured the Failing as wetlands endure most things: by already being poor in promises and rich in mud. Their tenants on the Reedfen margin fared worse, and remembered who had not warned them; that memory is a living politics, and its heirs appear in [[IX. The Rekindled Hearths]].
- **The Sepulchral Synod** endures, terraces and Provost and silence entire. The chalklands admit no census-takers. I say of my patrons only what the roads say: the caravans still cross the dry country, still need no water, and pay their tolls now in seed-grain, which the hungry margins accept and do not question aloud.

## The Ashfall — RR 2313

Two years into the Quiet, with the wars guttering for want of anything left to pledge, came the catastrophe the living remember when they say *the world ended* — for the Quiet beggared the world, but the Ashfall burned it. In high summer of RR 2313, fire took the uplands of the Middle Reach and the west: not wildfire, though it fathered wildfires, but a burning that fell and flowed, that took stone as readily as forest, that turned the sky the color of a dirty seal for three years of failed harvests. On the coasts it came differently and worse: the sea itself burned in patches, and where burning water met sand it left sheets and ropes of glass, and the tide brings glass ashore on those coasts to this hour.

Its cause carries more claimed authors than any event I have recorded, and I will honor my vow one last bitter time and resolve nothing: a Covenant deep-forge breached in the sealed mountains; a Thaumic great work, half-built through the whole late war by one power or several, loosed unfinished by the Failing of its restraints; the Drowned Star's partisans say the star, waking; the hearth-faiths say the lease, called in. I record what every account shares: it began in no country's heartland and spared no country's border, and no herald ever came forward to claim it, which in twenty-three centuries of recorded war had never once been true of any deliberate fire.
