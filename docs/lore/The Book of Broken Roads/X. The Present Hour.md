> Maps and promises have become valuable again. Everything in this book is the price history charged to make that sentence possible.

It is the year 2447 of a reckoning only I still keep. I write this final chapter in the tower at Grey Weir, above a ford where wagons cross again after a century of silence, and I record the present hour as I have recorded every hour: as evidence, for those who come after. This is the world as it stands — the world in which the reader now acts.

## The shape of the hour

- **Hearth caravans** go out each spring past the edge of the drawn maps, whole households under canvas, to found what their charters gravely call settlements and their mothers call graves-in-waiting. Some prove out. More every year.
- **Wayfinders** — the new age's scouts, half surveyor and half burglar — sell what the Roads once gave: knowledge of what lies past the next ruined station. Their maps are copied, forged, stolen, and killed over. I have authenticated maps for three parties to the same murder, and the reader now knows everything worth knowing about the map trade.
- **Oath guards** stand at the new markets and the sworn boundaries, and the title is exact, and no one who uses it knows why it makes me stand still a moment. They guard oaths. It is a beginning.
- **Public ledgers** — 'civic accounts,' the Compact's clerks call them — spread from market to market, open books in open squares, reconnecting bargain to bargain the slow way, by daylight and audit, as the stones once did in the dark for free. It is slower. It may — one entry of judgment, at the end of the book, as an old clerk's fee — it may be sounder.
- **The fourteen springs** run on, indifferent to the ages; the hedge-teachers are becoming schools again, the schools will become colleges, and colleges, as this book has shown at length, become estates of power. The traditions and disciplines the new mages will fight over are set out in the [[Appendix — The Fourteen Traditions]]. Nothing in that appendix has changed since the Unwritten World. Everything else has, twice.

## What the hour will become

I am asked to prophesy, in the towns that beg me back. I refuse; prophecy is motive-writing about the future. But a clerk may extrapolate, and I will close the book with the extrapolation I give the councils that ask honestly. The rekindled powers are entering an age of fracture — small worlds, jealous borders, everything to found and everything to lose. If it runs as the last cycle ran, fracture will breed crowns, and crowns will breed a reckoning, and some clerk not yet born will write the whole bill out again under a colder title. **It is not required to run as the last cycle ran.** That is the entire yield of twenty-three centuries of records, and I set it down as the finding of this book: the Failing was not fate but bookkeeping, entry by entry, each one chosen, each one attested, each one refusable at the time by the people who did not refuse it.

Five roads out of the fracture are visible already from my tower, and I name them as a wayfinder names passes, without blessing any: dominion, by which the strong make the quarrels stop; hegemony, by which the cunning make the quarrels theirs; prosperity, by which the diligent make the quarrels unaffordable; legacy, by which the patient outlast them; and ascendancy, by which someone — there is always someone; see [[VI. The Reckoning]] — reaches past the fourteen springs for the machinery underneath. Against that last road this book is my only levy, and I have made it as heavy as I could.

---

Here my record ends, not because the story ends but because the present is not the chronicler's country. What happens next belongs to the living: to the councils and the marches, the caravans and the wayfinders, the grain-quarrels and the drainage-quarrels and the quiet stones watching the new banners pass. Act, then, knowing what it cost to win back a world where promises can be kept — and when your own age closes, whatever it has made of itself, see that somebody honest writes it down. There will be another book. Let it be a **book of rekindled hearths**, and let it be true, and if its writer needs the old ledgers, the tower at Grey Weir keeps a light. It is only a candle at the door. I told you the custom was useless. I never said it was unwelcome.

> Sealed at Grey Weir, RR 2447, by Ilthen Vael, Third Recorder, struck from the rolls — the record continuing.
