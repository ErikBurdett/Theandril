> The Roads did not carry goods. Goods went by cart. The Roads carried the reason men dared load the cart.

It is already hard, one long lifetime after the Failing, to make the young understand what the Witness Roads *were*. They hear 'roads' and think of paving. Let me be exact, as an old clerk is entitled to be.

## What the Roads were

A waystation was a tolled crossing — a ford, a pass, a harbor mouth — with a sealed waystone at its heart and a chapter of Witnesses in residence. A traveler paid the toll in grain, coin, or recitation. A party to a bargain paid more, and got more: testimony taken under rite, the bargain cut into wax and lead, the seal registered at the stone, and — this was the whole of it — **the bargain attested at every other stone on the network.** A grain factor in the Anvilheights could strike terms with a fleet owner of the Outer Isles, neither having met the other, each trusting not the man but the lattice. Credit crossed mountains. Brides and hostages and treaties crossed. Peace, of a working kind, crossed.

The mechanism of attestation is the great disputed matter of all Theandril's history, and I give the dispute honestly. The doctrine of my own order held that the stones **recorded**: that a seal cut under rite was impressed into the lattice as a mark is pressed into wax, mindless and exact. The folk conviction, never once dislodged by doctrine, held that the stones **listened**: that the recited dead attended the stones, and that it was the dead who carried word from ford to ford, tireless couriers in the dark, and that this was why bargains were spoken *to* the stone with the ancestors' names called first. I sat a stone for twenty-one years. I took ten thousand testimonies. I felt — I will permit myself this single unclerkly sentence in the whole of this book — I felt *attended*. Make of it what you will. Both doctrines fit every fact I possess, including the facts of the Failing.

## What the Roads made possible

- **Distance without treachery.** The hearthlands knitted into one commercial world. The great fairs rose at the junction stations; the junction stations became towns; the towns, cities.
- **Credit.** A sealed bargain could be pledged against another. This began honestly, as seed-corn loans. Hold the thought, for it is the worm in the apple, and it eats through [[IV. The Age of Crowns]] to the end.
- **Law.** Erasure became excommunication from commerce itself. To be struck from the stones was civil death, and the fear of it governed better than any army.
- **The Witnessry's estate.** Four hundred stations' tolls made my order rich, and richer than rich: *necessary*. We swore neutrality to every crown and were mostly believed, and mostly deserved it, for longer than most orders keep any oath at all.

The raising ran eight centuries, and I mark the Age of First Oaths as ending when the last blank space on the network closed — when a bargain could travel from the far Anvilheights to the southern chalk without leaving attested ground. After that, the world the Roads had made began to make powers worthy of it. Their story is [[IV. The Age of Crowns]]. The price of it all is [[VI. The Reckoning]].
