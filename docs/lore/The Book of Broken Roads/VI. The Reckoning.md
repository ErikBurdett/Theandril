> The last age of the old world spent twenty years proving that everything the stones held could be spent as a weapon, and the stones held everything.

Historians who came before me liked clean beginnings for wars. I am a clerk; I give the reader entries, in order, and the shape assembles itself. The Reckoning ran, by my accounting, from RR 2291 to the Failing in 2311 — twenty years in which the powers of [[IV. The Age of Crowns]] turned the machinery of trust into ordnance and fired it at one another until the machinery itself gave way.

## The War of Written Fire — RR 2291–2298

It began as a paper war. A cache of counterfeit seals — true-dressed, decades deep — was uncovered in the Anchorage in RR 2291, implicating League houses, Throne customs officers, and, catastrophically, two chapters of the Witnessry. Every power in the world held pledges that might now be false; every power moved at once to enforce, redeem, or repudiate. The stones' lattice, asked simultaneously to prove forty times the world's worth of promises, answered with the sublime indifference of stone: every *lawfully sworn* bargain stood, forged provenance and all. The phrase 'written fire' is from a League pamphlet of 2293: *they have set fire to us in writing.* Armies marched on ledger entries. The Debt-Crowned called in three generations of mortgaged oaths and drowned half the coast's houses in a season. Erasure — the old, holy punishment — was handed out wholesale by partisan chapters, and for the first time in two thousand years men learned to *prefer* erasure, to seek it, because a man struck from the stones owed nothing. Within five years the world contained, for the first time, a class of soldiers with no attested existence. They fought accordingly.

## The Severing — RR 2298–2309

In RR 2298, Warden-King **Ossric, third of the name**, of the Null Throne, did the thing no crown in twenty-three centuries had done: he ordered a waystation killed. Covenant-forged null-anchors were set at the four corners of the station at Reed Ford, and the stone was silenced — not broken, *silenced*, cut from the lattice like a name from a recitation. Every bargain routed through Reed Ford lost attestation in an hour. The Throne's declaration named it surgery: the cutting of corrupted credit from the body of the world. I copied that declaration with these hands, and I copy here what the toll ledgers show instead: every station Ossric severed, in eleven years of surgery, stood on a route that fed his enemies' credit, and none on his own.

The precedent, once set, ran like ash-lung through a toll-town. The League severed stations to void its called debts. Covenant holds severed their own passes to seal the mountains. Free cities severed stations rather than see them enforced against them. Each severed stone dumped its held bargains into dispute, and each dispute fed the armies. By RR 2309, of the four hundred stations of the network's height, perhaps two hundred and sixty still spoke. The lattice held — the Roads' builders had built past all praise — but it held as a wounded man holds: by routing everything through what remained.

## The Hollowing — RR 2305–2311

Last, and worst, and I set it down flatly because there is no way to set it down otherwise. In the starving middle of the war, powers began to draw on the one collateral the stones had held for centuries and no one had dared call: **the pledged dead.** The Synod called in soul-collateral on defaulted bargains at scale — lawfully, I am bound to record, by the letter of instruments freely sworn in greeder generations — and marched the yield. Others, with none of the Synod's law and less of its craft, simply *harvested*: battlefield rites of the Forbidden traditions, whole necropolises drafted, the recited ancestors of erased men taken as salvage on the argument that no attested kin remained to object. Whether the stones' own attending dead — if the stones had attending dead; the reader knows my discipline by now — were drawn upon, conscripted, or consumed, I cannot attest. I record only this correlation, and I have checked it across every surviving ledger in my keeping: from RR 2305, stations began to fail *that no anchor had touched* — going first faint, the seals attesting slower, the recitations echoing thinner, and then quiet. The folk word for those six years is the Hollowing. The folk word is better than any doctrine I have.

> [!warning]
> Casualty rolls for the Reckoning do not exist, and I will not invent them. I can attest what I counted from the Grey Weir tower in the war's last autumn: the Sallow ran with pyre-ash for nineteen days, and the toll-road walked with refugees from first light to last, and my station's stone had gone so faint that their bargains — for they still stopped, even then, even starving, to swear their little bargains — could no longer be attested past the next ford. I sealed them anyway. Some things one seals anyway.
