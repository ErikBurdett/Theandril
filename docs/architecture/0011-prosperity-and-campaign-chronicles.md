# 0011 — First victory and complete campaign chronicles

Status: accepted for the next implementation slice; not a claim of 1.0 completion.

The simulation owns separate mundane knowledge, civic institutions and military doctrine, and a first Prosperity victory project. Its publicly announced settlement location creates an objective opponents can blockade or capture. Technology is not caster capability. The other four intended victory paths and magical advancement remain unfinished.

Campaign duration is a product requirement, not a fast-test artifact: normal play should last hundreds of turns, with higher-difficulty campaigns potentially approaching 1,000. A canonical, saved pace selection separates the short regression/skirmish profile from Standard, Long and Epic campaigns. Profiles scale late knowledge requirements, the economic commitment and the public project response window, not a minimum-turn lock or hidden AI resource gifts. Difficulty personalities, asymmetry, additional strategic systems and long-game engagement still need development; selecting pace alone is not evidence of completed difficulty design. Actual duration is measured through AI play and can change through conquest or economic loss.

The existing 200-event feed and 20-report battle ledger are intentionally bounded read models, not a full campaign record. A passive `@theandril/chronicle` recorder wraps the same command API used by humans and AI. It preserves the initial snapshot, every submitted command and result, emitted events, completed tactical reports, and end-turn hash checkpoints. It never determines game rules. A versioned, checksummed persistence envelope stores the canonical snapshot and this archive together, including the controller mode. Existing raw saves remain importable but are explicitly marked as having history only from import onward; missing history is never invented.

After canonical victory, the recorder produces two documents: a machine-readable technical log with replay material, and an original deterministic history tome grounded in recorded facts. Routine movement/production is summarized by turn in the tome; the technical record retains exact orders. Archive data is requested explicitly after victory, not transferred in ordinary observation deltas. It is not available as an omniscient mid-game intelligence source.

AI-watch runs bounded rounds through ordinary filtered observations and commands, with pause/resume and single-step controls. It stops on the same victory rule as a human campaign. The browser does not invent a winner or rewrite history. Full archive storage has the existing 64 MiB save limit; failures remain visible and cannot evict previous valid saves. Long-run archive chunking is a future scale optimization, not silent truncation.

Archive validation checks shape, sequence, turn transitions, complete-history origin, mandatory completed battle reports, the latest available checkpoint, and the final victory seal. Explicit replay verifies every result, event and tactical report. Mid-turn archive provenance is not authenticated merely by the envelope checksum: checksums detect accidental corruption, not malicious rewriting of the snapshot and archive together. Commands are recorded only through the worker wrapper; a recorder exception stops further orders until recovery. Save/export limits count UTF-8 bytes, matching the gzip decoder.

Verification includes generated-start AI victory, full-history retention beyond the feed cap, replay and resumed-archive equivalence, save corruption/recovery, real UI victory/log download, narrow-screen and keyboard dialog behavior, and separate canonical/archive performance measurements.
