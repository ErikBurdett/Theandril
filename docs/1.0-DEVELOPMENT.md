### M1 — Client states and unification victory

**State:** in progress. The Unification victory (rules 19, now a two-thirds bid) is delivered; rules 21 seats independent city-states; and rules 22 adds paid client obligations whose hearths stand behind a patron's bid, so unification can be negotiated as well as conquered. Client capture outcomes, permitted transfers between patron and client, and losing a bid to politics rather than arms remain pending. **Owners:** sim/protocol/content, AI,
worker/read models and diplomacy/conquest/victory UI.

- Add paid, validated proposals for client/tributary relationships, acceptance,
  refusal and permitted settlement outcomes. Make obligations, duration,
  independence/breach conditions and existing-war effects visible before consent.
  Prevent self-rule, relationship cycles and duplicate/conflicting obligations.
- Extend the existing conquest choices with client rule and lawful transfers;
  preserve eligible liberation, resettlement, population, production, ownership,
  worked-land and fog behavior. Explain unavailable options in the real UI.
- Give diplomatic AI explainable reasons to accept, resist, renegotiate or break
  obligations, using only known strength, grievances and costs. Client income or
  support must come from actual resources, not a free duplicate economy.
- Implement unification as a second victory with visible, stable objectives and
  a contest period. Both conquered holdings and valid political relationships
  have explicit eligibility; losing territory or clients can interrupt
  progress. Keep it mechanically distinct from accumulating Prosperity.

**Acceptance:** a human and an AI can create and resist a client relationship,
experience and inspect its obligations, end it legally, and contest a unification
attempt. Tests cover cycles, refusal/expiry, insufficient funds, defeat,
hidden information and capture-boundary save/replay. M5 later integrates ruler
succession and legitimacy with those obligations. A real browser
journey reaches the new victory and another prevents it. Preserve historical
Prosperity archives. Two playable paths advance Gate B but do not satisfy its
minimum of three.

### M2 — A complete magical discovery loop

**State:** in progress. Rules 23 delivers the discovery half — seams hidden under Ashfall glass, a paid survey, control and extraction, and Arcane Theory gated on a held seam — and rules 24 closes the loop with Unbinding, its saved interruption, and cultures whose traditions differ in what they can reach and what their theory costs. Reactive interception during an enemy cast and personal path growth remain. **Owners:**
sim/content/mapgen, AI, character/research/map UI and filtered site observations.

- Generate hidden magical sites and a paid search action. Discovery reveals only
  permitted information; control/extraction produces an actual resource and a
  reason to protect or contest the site.
- Connect a site-gated discovery, Arcane Theory research and a named caster's
  distinct aptitudes/growth. The UI answers who can use an unlock and which
  research, capability, geography and costs are still missing.
- Deliver one complete effect/counter-effect pair through ordinary commands,
  including battle or strategic consequences, interruption and a paid response.
  Show two authored faction approaches with materially different access or costs.
- Teach AI to search, research, qualify/select a caster, budget the effect and
  counter an observed threat. Avoid a parallel magic-only economy or rule engine.

**Acceptance:** player and AI discover a site, obtain its resource, research and
use an effect with a capable caster, and prevent or remove an opposing effect.
An incapable caster is rejected despite national research. Costs, interruption,
fog, aptitude progression and counterplay survive saves and command replay.
Browser coverage uses actual controls, including clear unavailable reasons.
This loop is not acceptance of the full magical content or Gate F2.

## Design defaults when the brief leaves room

Borrow design principles, not names, prose, artwork or complete subsystems:

- **Total War: Warhammer III:** distinctive army roles, legible battlefield intent,
  terrain/composition/leadership tradeoffs, strategic consequences and counterplay.
- **Civilization:** understandable opportunity costs, useful empire-level management,
  visible progress toward genuinely different, contestable victory paths.
- **Songs of Syx:** households, production, logistics and shortages that explain
  themselves at settlement and realm scale; depth should reduce tedious clicking.
- **Stellaris:** purposeful long-term goals, differentiated societies, escalation and
  late-campaign decisions rather than multiplied waiting costs.
- **Europa Universalis:** durable diplomatic/war objectives, credible commitments,
  geography and logistics; expose consequences before the player commits.

These are direction-setting references, not claims of fresh competitor playtests.
Translate them into Theandril's hearths, cultures, terrain, doctrine and magic.
A named inspiration is not permission for an enormous rewrite or another 1.0 gate.
Prefer a small coherent playable loop with AI counterplay to a catalog of buttons.
