## City-states and wider realms

Twenty-four city-states now hold the land between the great realms. Each is one settlement that never joined anything: it borrows a neighbouring culture's art, crafts and troops, and keeps only its own name, banner colour, motto and the particular bargain it refuses to sign. A city-state founds nothing further, opens no war and pursues no victory, but it builds, defends its hearth, holds territory and answers envoys like any other power. Their register and histories are published in the lore library.

Realms also run wider. Planting a hearth costs less as a realm grows, civic borders fill in a handful of turns instead of dozens, and administration and territory upkeep are gentler, so a campaign ends with far more of the map settled. A world can now seat up to forty-two realms alongside its city-states; seats past the authored cultures take their own epithet and banner colour, and their borrowed artwork is washed in it, so no two banners on a crowded map look alike.

Saved campaigns from the previous rules load unchanged, keep their exact bytes and replay from their archives.
