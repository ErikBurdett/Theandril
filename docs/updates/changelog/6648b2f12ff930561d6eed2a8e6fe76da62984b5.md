## Somewhere to put the stores

Supply follows your hearths, which meant that until now the only way to reach further was to take someone else's — the very thing supply was supposed to make expensive. March an army into open country, or put one ashore on a coast you do not own, and there was nothing you could do about it except found a town.

Now a company can stop and raise a depot where it stands. Forty coin and the rest of its movement, and the ground two hexes around it is fed — four hexes along a road. That is half of what a hearth reaches, which is enough to carry an advance forward one bound at a time, and not enough to make distance stop mattering.

They are not free to keep. Each one draws two coin a turn alongside every other obligation, so a long chain of them is a real decision about what else you are not buying. They also stand apart — three hexes between any two of yours, thirty-two at most — so what you build is a line of posts rather than a blanket.

And they can be taken from you. Any enemy company that walks onto a depot pulls it down at the end of the turn, and both sides hear about it. A raiding party that slips behind your army and sits on the post it is eating from now does something that a battle cannot: it does not beat your army, it strands it.

So there are two ways to sustain a war now, and they cost different things. Take the next hearth, and it feeds you as it fed its old owner. Or build your own line forward, pay for it every turn, and keep something behind you worth guarding.
