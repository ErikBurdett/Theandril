## Oaths between realms

A realm can now take another realm as its client. The patron pays a subsidy when the oath is accepted; the client pays tribute every turn for an agreed term, keeps its own hearths, armies and orders, and neither realm may make war on the other while the obligation holds. Every term — what is paid now, what is owed each turn, how long it binds, and what it forbids — is stated before either side consents.

The oath can end three ways. A patron may release its client at any time. A client may walk away lawfully once the term has run. Or it may renounce early, which is a breach: the binding peace between the two realms ends and the broken word is remembered.

This changes how a campaign can be won. A client's hearths stand behind its patron's unification bid, so the second victory path is now open to diplomacy as well as conquest — and city-states, who have no wars of their own to fight, make willing clients. Rival realms court them, and the Victory tab shows how many hearths are your own and how many were pledged to you.

Because patrons and clients keep out of each other's wars, campaigns ran longer than intended, so the two longest paces are re-priced from measured games. Saved campaigns from the previous rules load unchanged and replay exactly.
