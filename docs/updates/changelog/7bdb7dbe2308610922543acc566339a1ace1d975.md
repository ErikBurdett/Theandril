## A hearth that answers for itself

The bigger your realm got, the longer your turn took. Forty hearths meant forty visits, most of them to say the same thing again. That is the wrong way round, and it is what standing charters are for.

Give a hearth a charter and it places its own order whenever its queue runs empty. You set two things: what it reaches for first — Works, Wealth, Learning or Muster — and the most coin it may spend on any single order. It never spends past that ceiling, it always leaves forty coin in the treasury, and it waits until wages are paid before it buys anything.

A charter is not an autopilot you have to fight. It only acts on an empty queue, so anything you order by hand simply comes first and the charter waits. Revoke it and the hearth is back under direct orders, with nothing to undo.

It also tells you what it is about to do. The panel names the next work — *Next: Charter market* — or gives you the reason there isn't one: every building already stands there, the ceiling is too low, the treasury is near its reserve. That reason now replaces the generic nag in your next-action list, and a hearth whose charter is working drops out of that list altogether. What is left is the hearths that actually want a decision from you.

Rival realms use the same mechanism: when a realm grows wider than one turn of orders can cover, the hearths its planner never reached keep building on a charter of their own.
