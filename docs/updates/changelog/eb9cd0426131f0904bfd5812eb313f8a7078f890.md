## They will cut your line too

Supply posts have been destructible since they arrived: walk a company onto an enemy depot and it comes down at the end of the turn. Until now only you were doing it. Rival realms would build depots to keep their own armies fed, then march straight past yours without a glance, which made the sharpest thing about supply something only one side ever used.

They notice now. A rival company with fighting on its mind will take a depot that lies on its path, and an army of yours that was being fed by that post is suddenly wasting away in open country wondering what happened.

It is deliberately a small appetite. A depot has to be close — within three hexes — and it never outranks an enemy army standing in front of them. They will take what is on the way; they will not abandon a battle to chase a supply post across the map.

That restraint took a measurement to find. The first version of this had rival armies ranging eight hexes for a depot and valuing it above the enemy in front of them, and it turned wars into a stalemate of mutual demolition — campaigns ran a fifth longer while both sides rebuilt the same posts. Pulled back to something opportunistic, the raiding still happens constantly and the war still ends when it should.
