## Distance finally costs something

An army twelve hexes inside someone else's realm used to fight exactly as well as one standing in its own capital. It rested as well, it recovered as fast, and it could stay there forever. Nothing in the game made a deep invasion harder than a shallow one.

Hearths now feed the ground around them — four hexes over open country, and twice as far along a road you have built. That is your supply, and it moves with your hearths rather than your borders. Roads are no longer only about marching speed; they are how a realm reaches.

It can be cut. An enemy company standing on a hex stops supply passing through it. A hearth under siege feeds nobody at all. So there is now something to do with a fast company besides fight: get behind an enemy army and sit on the road it is eating from.

A force left outside supply loses four strength a turn — down to a fifth of its full strength, never further — and rests badly, recovering morale and fatigue at a third of the usual rate. It does not collapse. It simply falls behind an enemy that is being fed, which is what eventually decides the fight.

None of this is a tax on exploring. A force of fewer than three companies forages for itself, so scouts and raiding parties go where they like. So does any expedition carrying a caravan, so settling is never punished. And a realm that holds no hearth at all — every realm on turn one — has no line to cut in the first place.

The shape this gives a war is the point. You cannot simply march to someone's capital any more. You take the border hearth first, because a captured hearth feeds *you*, and then the next one. Your selected force will tell you which hearth is feeding it, or what standing outside is costing it, and anything starving is called out in the end-of-turn list under its own heading.
