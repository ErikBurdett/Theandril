## Supply reaches the water

Until now supply walked, and it stopped at the tideline. That produced two odd answers. A fleet was fed wherever it happened to be, including halfway across an ocean, on the reasoning that ships carry their own stores — which is true for a week and nonsense over a campaign. And an army put ashore on your own coast, close enough to see a harbour across the strait, counted as out of supply until it spent forty coin building a post on the beach.

Build a harbour and that hearth now carries its line out over the water. Same reach, same roads, same enemies able to sit on it and cut it — but a line that starts at a harbour can cross the sea, and one that does not still stops at the shore. A landing within reach of a friendly port is simply fed.

It is worth saying what this does not do yet. Fleets are still exempt: a hull at sea is supplied wherever it is, and harbours extend supply to the water without yet requiring anyone to stay inside it. Taking that exemption away changes how naval wars are fought, so it is getting its own attention rather than being smuggled in here.

Harbours were, until this, only a place to build ships. They are now also how a realm reaches past its own coastline.
