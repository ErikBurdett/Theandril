## You can see it now, and so can they

Supply has decided every campaign since it arrived, and until now you could not see it. The ground your hearths and depots can feed is drawn straight onto the map — a pale outline under everything else, on by default, and switchable off from the map guide if you would rather read it from the panels.

Depots can also be pulled down. When they arrived they came with upkeep and no way to stop paying it short of an enemy taking the post off you, which was simply a mistake. Stand a company on your own depot and you can dismantle it; the upkeep stops that moment and the ground it fed goes quiet again.

The larger change is that rival realms now play supply too. A realm at war whose army is wasting away outside its lines will stop and raise a depot under it rather than give up the campaign. It pays for it, it keeps paying for it, and you can go and pull it down.

That last part took three attempts, and the first two were abandoned on the belief that a competent AI made campaigns finish too quickly. That turned out to be a misreading of our own tests: the fast ones play a cramped four-realm map, while the length we actually aim for describes a full twelve-realm world. Measured properly, that campaign now runs 388 turns — right where it is meant to sit — with nothing about the game's balance changed to get there.
