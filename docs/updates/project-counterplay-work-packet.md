## One chance before the treaty

A realm can be one turn from completing its prosperity project while an enemy company stands beside the host, able to interrupt it. A binding peace at that moment can decide the race. In a retained authored regression, the defender could establish an ordinary siege immediately, yet its AI proposed or accepted ten turns of white peace because the war had lasted long enough. The project then completed on the next turn.

The candidate policy protects that narrow available response. If a currently visible enemy project can finish during the proposed truce and a suitable uncommitted army can legally establish a siege now, the AI can reject the incoming peace and make that interruption. With no incoming offer, it can prioritize the same immediate siege before an outgoing peace proposal. It does not infer an attack opportunity from the public announcement alone or plan a distant invasion.

There is a meaningful reason to take peace instead: a strictly sooner active project of its own. A separate authored control gives both realms paid, progressing projects, with the defending realm one turn from its own victory. That control keeps the decision from becoming a blanket refusal to negotiate whenever a rival has a project.
