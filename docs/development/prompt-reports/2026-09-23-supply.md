# Supply lines: the first slice of M4 — 2026-09-23

## Prompt

“continue”, opening M4 — *sustain and disrupt land and sea wars through supply, trade and treaties*. This is the supply half of its first bullet: hearths support armies, and distance, geography and enemies impose visible costs.

## What was missing

Distance cost nothing. An army twelve hexes inside an enemy realm fought exactly as well as one standing in its own capital, recovered morale and fatigue at the same rate, and could stay there forever. Nothing in the game made a deep invasion harder than a shallow one, which meant there was no reason to take a border hearth before pushing on, no reason for roads beyond movement, and no way to hurt an enemy army except by fighting it.

## Delivered — rules/save 27

- **Supply spreads from hearths.** Every hearth feeds the ground around it: four hexes over open country, and twice as far along a built road. Supply is derived from the map every time it is asked — nothing is stored, and no save grew.
- **It can be cut.** An enemy company standing on a hex stops supply passing through it, a foreign hearth blocks it, and a hearth under siege feeds nobody at all. Raiding a supply line is now a real thing to do.
- **Outside it, a force wastes away.** Each turn out of supply a company loses four strength — down to a fifth of its full strength and no further — and recovers morale and fatigue at a third of the ordinary rate. It falls behind a fed enemy rather than collapsing outright.
- **Supply is the price of massing a force, not a tax on exploring.** A force of fewer than three companies forages for itself, as does any expedition carrying a caravan, as does every army of a realm that holds no hearth at all — which is every realm's opening.
- **A captured hearth feeds you.** Because supply follows hearths rather than borders, taking a border town is what lets an invasion continue. That is the strategic shape the system is for.
- **It says what it costs.** The selected force names the hearth that feeds it, or what standing outside supply is costing it, and a starving force is named in the turn list under its own heading — *1 company out of supply* — ahead of ordinary idleness.

## Measured, and two things the measurement changed

The first cut applied attrition to any army of two or more companies outside supply, with no recovery at all. Both were wrong, and the campaign suite said so rather than a review:

- **Expansion stopped.** A caravan with an escort is two companies, so colonising expeditions starved on the road and the AI founded one town instead of many. Settling expeditions and small forces now forage.
- **Wars stopped resolving.** With no recovery at all, an unsupplied army's morale fell to one and stayed there, so attacks failed and no realm reached a victory inside its pacing window. Reduced recovery keeps the penalty real without making a force useless.

I also tried teaching the AI to march worn, unsupplied armies home. It made things worse — campaigns ran past their turn caps because wars never finished — so it is reverted and recorded below as not done.

## Not done

- The AI does not plan around supply: it neither avoids campaigning outside it nor raids to cut an enemy's. It copes because the penalty is survivable.
- Fleets carry their own stores and are never out of supply; ports and depots do not exist yet.
- There is no map overlay for supply reach — the selected force names its hearth, but you cannot see the line.
- Attrition can never destroy a force, and supply carries no cost in coin or resources; trade routes, tax policy and treaty access are the rest of M4.
