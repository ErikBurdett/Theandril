# Standing charters: the first slice of empire delegation — 2026-09-22

## Prompt

“okay continue”, taking the next item on the road to 1.0 after rules 24 closed M2's counter. M3 is *empire delegation and coordinated orders* — govern a large realm without hand-ordering every hearth.

## What was missing

A settlement's entire production state was a five-item queue and one command that appends to it. Nothing repeated, nothing was delegated, and the only way to keep forty hearths working was to visit all forty every turn. The alert list made that worse rather than better: `actionCandidates` flagged every hearth with an empty queue, with no way to say *this one answers for itself*, so a wide realm produced a long list of identical nags and the recommended workflow was to cycle through all of them.

## Delivered — rules/save 25

- **A charter is a standing order, not an override.** A hearth may hold one charter. It places an order only when the queue is empty, so anything ordered by hand takes precedence and simply delays it. Revoking returns the hearth to direct orders.
- **Bounded by a ceiling and a reserve.** A charter never spends more than its ceiling on a single work (4–64 coin, set per hearth) and never draws the treasury below 40 coin. It is answered after upkeep is paid, so a policy can never starve a wage.
- **Four focuses over one candidate set.** Works, Wealth and Learning all finish the same hearth; the focus only says what it reaches for first, and ties fall to the cheaper work, so a charter never leaves a hearth half-built. Muster raises the most capable company the ceiling allows, and never a caravan or a hull.
- **It does nothing a player could not.** A charter goes through the ordinary production rules — requirements, coastal and technology gates, the five-item cap, the ordinary coin cost — and a hearth that changes hands ends its charter rather than serving a conqueror.
- **It always says what it will do next, or why it cannot.** The next work, or one of five plain refusals, is derived on demand rather than stored, so no explanation is ever stale in a save, a replay or the chronicle.
- **Alerts answer for themselves.** A hearth under a working charter no longer appears in the next-action list at all. One that has stalled appears with the charter's own reason in place of the generic “empty production queue”, which is the difference between a list of nags and a list of decisions.
- **The AI delegates when it outgrows a turn.** A realm with more idle hearths than one turn's orders can reach leaves the remainder a Works charter, so a hearth the planner never visited still builds itself out. It never charters Muster: a standing order must not raise companies whose upkeep the planner has not budgeted.

## A latent cycle, found and closed

Adding a schema to `save.ts` exposed a real import cycle: `save.ts` took `MAX_EVENTS` from `simulation.ts` while `simulation.ts` took `MAX_FACTIONS` back from `save.ts`. `MAX_FACTIONS` is only read inside a function, but `MAX_EVENTS` bounds a schema built at module scope — so any entry point that reached `simulation` first left that bound `undefined` and every save failed validation with an unreadable error. `MAX_EVENTS` now lives beside `MAX_FACTIONS` in `save.ts`, and the constants a schema needs flow one way only.

## Not done

- Charters cover production only. Army grouping, rally and standing roles — the other half of M3 — are still one command per army.
- Alerts are filtered and explained but not yet grouped by cause: forty stalled charters are still forty rows.
- A charter cannot be copied to several hearths at once, and there is no realm-wide default.
