# The budget the planner offered twice — 2026-09-23

## Prompt

“continue”, taking the last hand-wave in supply: fleets were exempt from it entirely. The attempt found a different bug, and the attempt itself did not ship.

## Delivered — no rules change

- **The AI no longer offers the same coin twice.** A turn's plan pays for an arcane survey, a supply depot and a patronage subsidy *before* it works out the budget for everything else — and that budget was computed from the treasury without subtracting any of them. The planner could therefore commit coin it had already spent, and the simulation refused the order.
- The survey has leaked twenty-four coin a turn since rules 23 and the depot forty since rules 28; neither had surfaced, because the budget usually had enough slack to absorb it. It surfaced the moment a change moved campaign trajectories, which is the least convenient moment for a latent accounting bug to matter.

## What did not ship, and why

Fleets are supplied wherever they are. The intended fix was provisions: a fleet works a fixed number of turns beyond its realm's reach, then wastes until it returns inside a harbour's supply. It was built, tested and measured, and then reverted.

The reason is the standard I set for myself two slices ago — the headline twelve-realm campaign, not the fast four-realm proxy, is what the 350–400 turn target describes:

| Provision turns | Headline campaign |
|---|---|
| None (shipped behaviour) | 388 |
| 8 | 427 |
| 16 | 412 |

Provisioning lengthens campaigns because naval invasions fail, and they fail partly because **the AI does not plan around fleet supply at all** — it dispatches expeditions that now starve, rather than staging through harbours. That is the same trap as the first two attempts at AI land supply: the measured cost is not the mechanic, it is the AI not yet understanding the mechanic. Dialling provisions up until the number fits would leave a rule that barely bites and an AI still unable to play it.

Shipping it honestly needs one of two things, and both are their own slice:

1. **AI naval staging** — expeditions that route through friendly harbours and turn back when stores run low — measured until the headline campaign returns to band; or
2. **A deliberate Epic re-price** with the current pace pack frozen, the way rules 18, 21 and 22 each re-priced when a rules change moved campaign length.
